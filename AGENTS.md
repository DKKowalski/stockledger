# StockLedger repository instructions

## Product design direction

StockLedger uses restrained, editorial visual hierarchy. For marketing work, draw structural inspiration from this reference without copying its colors or brand identity:

https://dribbble.com/shots/27644229-AI-SaaS-Landing-Page-Design

### Copy discipline

Every piece of text must earn its place.

- Do not add decorative eyebrow text, kickers, overlines, pre-headings, or tiny uppercase labels above headings.
- Do not add subtitles that merely restate a heading.
- Do not fill empty space with explanatory marketing copy.
- Prefer one strong headline over several layers of weak text.
- Keep supporting copy short. Include it only when it explains an action, consequence, limitation, or unfamiliar concept.
- Status, validation, progress, accessibility, and legally required text are allowed.
- Existing copy is not automatically approved. Remove redundant copy when redesigning a screen.
- When uncertain whether text is useful, remove it.

### Visual hierarchy

A screen should generally contain:

1. One dominant headline.
2. One concise supporting paragraph, only when necessary.
3. The primary action or product interface.

Use spacing, scale, composition, and imagery to establish hierarchy instead of adding labels.

### Interaction and motion

- Do not add hover animation to cards, screenshots, illustrations, or other surfaces that are not interactive.
- Reserve hover feedback for elements a user can click, open, select, drag, or otherwise operate.
- Motion must clarify hierarchy, state, continuity, or cause and effect. Do not animate an element merely to make it feel active.
- Do not use progress lines, checkmarks, status dots, controls, or control-like visuals unless they represent real product state.
- Prefer one restrained entrance system over several unrelated animation styles on the same page.
- Text reveals must follow the actual rendered lines at each breakpoint. Do not animate a multi-line phrase as one oversized block.
- Keep text-reveal travel to roughly one line height. Avoid rotation and exaggerated offsets.
- Let the primary headline nearly settle before introducing its supporting copy or actions.
- Verify animation timing during the transition—not only its finished state—at desktop and mobile sizes.
- Align repeated components to a shared grid. Keep their padding, icon positions, heading baselines, and content edges consistent.

Before completing design work, compare it visually with the relevant reference and explicitly check for unnecessary text at desktop and mobile sizes.
